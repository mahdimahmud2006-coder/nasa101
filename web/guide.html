What the layers mean.

HH, HV and false-colour radar

HH and HV are two polarization channels of NISAR’s L-band radar. Their backscatter values describe the strength of the radar return. The false-colour composite combines channels to make spatial patterns easier to distinguish; its colours are not natural optical colours.

Reference difference and previous-visit difference

A reference difference compares the selected visit with a stated baseline. A previous-visit difference asks what changed since the preceding available observation. A small recent difference can coexist with a large reference difference. Neither alone proves damage or recovery.

Bangladesh’s current investigation baseline is its first observed visit. Nepal Surface & coherence uses August 19 as its explicit reference even when earlier observations are displayed.

CPR and candidate signatures

CPR is HV minus HH in decibels. It describes the relationship between the two channels. Low-return and brightening candidates use illustrative thresholds; they are clues for investigation rather than validated classes of water, vegetation or damage. The cutoff sensitivity chart shows how candidate area changes as the thresholds vary.

Coherence

Coherence describes radar phase consistency between two acquisitions. Water, vegetation changes, geometry and noise can all lower it. The native-resolution pair summaries describe the crop; they do not automatically describe a selected intensity footprint. Ground displacement requires further phase validation and correction.

Footprints, gaps and playback

Select a saved footprint, inspect a local neighborhood, or draw a polygon in Evidence dossiers. The intensity trace uses the same valid pixels across its observations. Playback visits only available acquisitions; a missing date is not a zero or an estimated image. A satellite outage cannot be repaired by downloading a pair that spans it.

Terrain, thermal and scenarios

NASADEM provides historical terrain context. Landsat thermal observations have their own acquisition dates, cloud screening and resolution. Rainfall scenarios change model assumptions to explore susceptibility; their scores are uncalibrated and do not predict flood depth, extent or probability.

Find NASA maps for a radar visit

In the Atlas, choose Find comparison data under What to check next. The panel uses the radar dates to look up Sentinel-1 water maps in Bangladesh or Landsat and Sentinel-2 vegetation disturbance maps in Nepal. Check the date offset and actual map coverage. A catalog match only identifies a scene to inspect.

From question to evidence.

  1. Choose a country and question. Open the appropriate study area and read its observation period.
  2. Play the visits. Compare measured images using their displayed scales; inspect the acquisition calendar for gaps.
  3. Select a footprint. Keep the physical location fixed while comparing both channels and reference differences.
  4. Test the explanation. Review terrain, optical matches, threshold sensitivity and alternative causes where available.
  5. Export the dossier. Share the selected evidence, source identifiers, footprint and limitations for follow-up review.

A practical output is a defensible location and question for field checking. The project has not established operational decision accuracy or measured user impact.

Sources stay attached to the observations.

NISAR GCOV provides radar intensity; GUNW provides paired coherence and phase-related measurements. Accepted observations are processed locally and served from saved products. The interface requires no runtime language model or live NASA download.

  • NASA–ISRO NISAR mission · radar observations.
  • NASA Earthdata / ASF DAAC · source granule discovery and access.
  • ASF NISAR availability · acquisition coverage and documented outages.
  • NASA Earthdata NASADEM · terrain context.
  • USGS Landsat surface temperature · thermal observations in the Nepal environmental workspace.
  • NASA Harmonized Landsat–Sentinel · matched optical reference products where available.

Per-case Methods & sources and exported dossiers include the accepted granules, formulas, processing identifiers and available reference context. Data availability and validation differ by study area.

Interpretation and provenance

Radar data are provisional. Physical drivers remain competing explanations unless independently supported. Missing rainfall or optical evidence remains unavailable. Public sources are selected under a non-Chinese provider/site requirement, with declared upstream lineage reviewed; this does not certify the origin of every contributor to a global auxiliary dataset.
